Angel Engine

TACTICAL GRID COMBAT · UNITY / C# · 7-PERSON TEAM · U-TAD · 2025–2026

For our final-year team project I took both ends of combat: designing the fights and writing the code that runs them. The result is a data-driven, grid-based combat system — every enemy shares one movement/attack backbone, every ability is authored as data instead of hard-coded logic, and every dangerous tile is telegraphed a beat before it actually hurts you.

My role was Lead Programmer & Combat Designer in a team of seven. This page walks through the systems in the order the game uses them — the grid, the abilities that write onto it, and the enemies and bosses that cast them — with real footage and screenshots from the build.

01

A Grid That Remembers Danger

GridManager builds a rectangular grid of Tile objects at runtime from just a width and a height. In the game that board is eight columns by four rows, split into two halves — blue tiles on the player's side, red tiles on the enemy's. Each tile tracks whether it is occupied and how dangerous it currently is.

When an ability targets a tile, it raises that tile's danger level before the damage actually lands. That window is the frame everything else reads from: enemies — and eventually players — use it to read the board and decide where to move. Danger is state on the grid itself, not a visual effect bolted on afterwards: the telegraph you see and the information the AI uses are the same data.

Abilities as Data, Not Code

AbilityV2 reads its behaviour entirely from an AbilityPattern data asset. Designing a new attack means filling in that asset, not touching ability code — one dispatcher reads the chosen shape and runs the matching routine. On the player side, abilities are equipped through an Abilities Build menu and fired from a four-slot ability bar on the HUD.

03

One Base Class, Three Very Different Bosses

EnemyBase handles shared movement, attacks, stun and confuse; every enemy — trash mob or boss — inherits from it. EnemyBoss adds phases and ally waves on top, and three subclasses only override what makes them different.

EnemyBase · extends HealthSystem

The shared backbone

Movement, attacks, stun and confuse live here once, so every enemy behaves consistently on the grid.

EnemyBoss · extends EnemyBase

Phases + ally waves

Adds HP-gated phases and the ability to bring in waves of allies — the hooks all three bosses build on.

Boss 01

Heraldo

Three HP-gated phases. Before every attack he mirrors the player's row or column, so the threat always comes from where you are standing.

Boss 02

Heraldo Celestial

Phase 3 is a permanent fusion — extra max HP and a wider five-move rotation, not just a new attack set.

Boss 03

Computer

Invulnerable until it spawns a minion wave, then only takes damage inside a timed window — miss it, and the fight resets.

Friction — Custom VALORANT Map

Solo level design project · April 2025

A two-site tactical map set in the hillside favelas of Rio de Janeiro, built around one question: what happens when one site sits far above the other? Kingdom Corporation is running radianite energy experiments deep under the neighborhood, and VALORANT's Protocol agents have moved in to shut it down — Site A sits on the high ground, Site B sits low, and mid is the only clean link between them.

The Mechanic — Elevators

Two elevators anchor mid as the map's main mechanic. They're loud and open up a whole new strategy — calling one tells the enemy team exactly where you are, in exchange for a shortcut and a fresh angle once the doors open. Once activated, an elevator can't be stopped or re-triggered until it finishes its run, so using one is a full commitment, not a quick peek.

From concept to Final form

The first paper sketch got the two-site, one-mid skeleton right, but not the details: several sightlines were wide enough to feel like an open arena rather than a tactical map, and the height changes weren't marked clearly enough to block out consistently. The build pass added walls to tighten those angles and cleaned up where stairs actually needed to sit.
